We used to measure onboarding in steps completed. Then we watched twenty
people do it, and learned that nobody experiences steps. They experience the
gap between arriving and the first moment something is unmistakably theirs.

For us that moment is the first piece of real content on a real screen. We
call it the first win, and we time it.

## Seven minutes, measured properly

The clock starts at account creation and stops at the first save that is not
sample data. When we started measuring, the median was 34 minutes and a third
of accounts never reached it at all.

The three biggest costs were not what we expected:

| Step                                        | Median time |
| ------------------------------------------- | ----------- |
| Choosing a name for the workspace           | 4m 10s      |
| Reading the field type list                 | 6m 40s      |
| Waiting for the first invite to be accepted | 11m         |

Nobody had ever complained about any of the three.

## What we changed

**We stopped asking for the workspace name.** It is derived from the email
domain and editable later. Four minutes of hesitation, removed by not asking a
question we could answer ourselves.

**We cut the field types shown at creation from twelve to four.** The other
eight are behind "More", and are used by fewer than one account in ten during
the first session. Choice is expensive when you do not yet know what you are
choosing between.

**We made the invite optional and asynchronous.** Waiting for a colleague is
not onboarding, it is a blocked task wearing onboarding's clothes.

## What we did not change

We did not add a product tour. We built one, tested it with eight people, and
watched six of them dismiss it within two seconds. The two who read it did not
finish faster.

We also kept the sample content. Removing it tested worse: people are more
willing to edit something that exists than to create something from nothing,
and the first edit is a smaller step than the first creation.

> Make the first action a change, not a creation.

## Where we are

Median time to first win is now 6m 48s, and 71% of accounts reach it in the
first session. The remaining tail is almost entirely people who arrive, look,
and come back the next day, which is not a problem to fix.
